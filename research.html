Research

I’m broadly interested in migration and American Politics research using causal inference and other quantitative methods.

My research primarily focuses on how immigration policy affects political behavior, attitudes, and identity in the United States. More specifically, I examine the relationships between state-level immigrant policy and outcomes such as immigrant political participation, linked fate, and policy preferences.

My current work examines how state-level immigrant integration policy features affect attitudes using original surveys, unique measurements of fairness, and pre-registered conjoint experiments. I test how policy attributes such as eligibility criteria and social service provision affect perceptions of fairness and policy support, finding little evidence for economic and cultural threat arguments. The work received the 2026 Pacific Chapter of the American Association for Public Opinion Research Student Paper Award and the UC Santa Barbara Manzer-Wesson Award for Best Conference Paper.

I show that Americans are more supportive of allocating social services to immigrants when a variety of law and security-based criteria are met. Moreover, I find very limited evidence that immigrants’ region of origin influences perceptions of fairness or policy support.

The collection of these original large surveys were supported by numerous internal and external grants and are the basis for ongoing projects addressing American immigration attitudes.
